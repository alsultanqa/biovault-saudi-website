## 1. Product intent
The site is not a static profile mirrored online. It is a guided experience for a Saudi institutional visitor: *understand the point of view → identify a relevant challenge → explore the appropriately caveated solution → start an executive briefing.*

**Primary visitors:** banking/financial institutions, investment and diligence teams, enterprise innovation/technology leaders. Secondary: future-of-work stakeholders and potential partnership organizations.  
**Commercial next step:** a focused 30-minute introductory conversation, not a request for an immediate sales commitment.  
**Current CTA implementation:** informational anchor + mailto addressed to the email printed in the approved profile, with **no collection server** and **no calendar booking integration**.

### Intent hierarchy
- First five seconds: “What can't your systems see today?” + institutional design and clear exploration path.
- First thirty seconds: the visibility gap, the BioVault question/evidence/test approach, a sector entry point and four service/product directions.
- Next 2–4 minutes: see a synthetic educational example of a concept and its limitations.
- End: ask to explore one defined challenge with BioVault.

### Copywriting guardrails
- Do not claim active Saudi bank clients, Saudi banking regulator approval, Saudi corporate registration, local hosting, data residency or quantified performance without independently approved documentation.
- Do not reproduce QDB/Doha Bank names, logos, financial details or deals in public site copy without appropriate evidence and permission.
- Do not call the BalanceChain demo a real ledger, a value-tracing implementation or a verified AML tool.
- Explain that Value Objects represent existing monetary value, not new sovereign currency or token, and that visibility ends at the supported observation perimeter.
- Do not imply EndlessJob is publicly launched or that all pictured workflows exist today.
- Distinguish “we explore a use case” from “we have delivered a client deployment.”

## 6. Saudi-market publishing and privacy gate
Saudi PDPL and SDAIA guidance should be reviewed before enabling inquiry forms, cookies, lead capture, analytics or third-party transfers. At minimum the publishing team needs to define what personal data is collected, why, how stored, who can access it, applicable retention, disclosure and rights notice; this is a design checklist, **not legal sign-off**. Official sources:
- `https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/` — SDAIA Data Governance Platform / personal data law guidance.
- `https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Pages/RegulationsAndPolicies.aspx` — SDAIA regulations and policies.

**Current preview deliberately avoids server-side form processing and external analytics.** No cookies are intentionally created by this prototype's JS. Avoid treating this as proof of production compliance.

## 8. Prototype behavior and QA
### Implemented and locally exercised
- English + Arabic text, browser RTL/LTR switch.
- Desktop navigation, mobile menu and sections.
- Sector tabs and product cards.
- Educational showroom tabs and 500-SAR split slider.
- No backend, no database, no cookies/analytics added and no public deployment.

### Local scripted checks run
- Render screenshots at 1440 px desktop and 390 px mobile; no JS page errors.
- Arabic language changed page direction to RTL; numeric slider updated.
- Four solutions and four sectors present.
- Document width equal to viewport for checked desktop/mobile layouts; no horizontal scroll.

### Incomplete before release
- Cross-browser + screen reader audit and full WCAG accessibility validation.
- Security testing, CSP/headers, external-link policy, copy/legal review.
- Production Next.js/TypeScript architecture, localization routing, CMS option and deployment pipeline.
- Privacy notice, form processing, consent management only if required by actual data flows.
- Official vector web assets and licenses; client permission and product maturity matrix.
- Company Saudi commercial/legal details and publicly valid contact channel.
